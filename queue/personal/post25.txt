[NAME: Curing stage — 80 planter columns]

Eighty of these. Straight out of the mould.

What you're looking at is the curing stage. No polishing yet. And this is exactly where the discipline shows.

The form is already there. Sharp. Consistent. Cast once, repeated eighty times — because the whole point of a mould is that the shape doesn't drift from piece one to piece eighty.

The colour? Ignore it for now. That variation is just curing. It disappears under polishing — smooth skin, even tone, all eighty reading as one set.

People assume "identical" is the easy option. It's the opposite. Identical takes one mould and total control of the pour. If you wanted eighty individual forms instead — every planter its own shape — that's eighty moulds. The tooling alone rewrites the timeline and the cost.

So the question is always the same up front: do you want a series, or eighty originals?

These become planters. Heading to site next.

Matched set, or one-of-a-kind — what would you choose for your project?
